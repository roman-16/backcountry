# Systems

Every mechanic of Backcountry, what it does for the player, and its edge cases. The Backcountry mod builds each system itself unless a mod is named. Numbers live in `balance.toml`; those quoted here are the first values to tune from.

## Settled

### S1 One body, counted in calories

The mod owns the survivor's whole body, so every number is in kcal, °C, kg and days, and the HUD and the Satellite Messenger read one model. No public survival mod goes in beside it.

- **Food** is counted in kcal, fat, protein, carbs and vitamin C, read from data in the mod for every food of every jar in the pack (`data/backcountry/data_maps/item/food.json`). An item with no entry is not food. Eating fills the stomach, which holds 3,072 kcal and digests 256 kcal an hour; a full stomach refuses more ("Too full to eat"). An empty stomach growls now and then until the survivor eats (Subtle Effects' growl, heard by that survivor alone).
- **A game day burns what a real day does:** every activity costs what the same game time of it costs a real person, a game day standing for a whole day. Standing costs 1.5 W a kg (the Pandolf equation's standing term, 99 kcal a game hour at 77 kg), and sitting on a slab or stair (Sit) costs the same, as the Compendium rates sitting and standing quietly alike; sleep 0.95 kcal a kg an hour. Walking adds the Pandolf equation's walking term at 1.34 m/s for the body and what it carries, the terrain multiplying it (soft snow 1.3 + 0.082 per cm of depth, to 50 cm, snowshoes sinking a quarter as deep; ice 1.7) and the slope by Minetti's cost of walking on gradients, to 45%; wind adds a quarter at 18 km/h on open ground. Running, swimming, climbing, chopping, digging, mining, building, fighting, the bow drill, cranking, dressing and scraping cost their MET from the Compendium of Physical Activities for the game time they fill: a block for as long as it takes to break, a dressing click for 4 game minutes, a bow-drill stroke for half a minute. Carried load also costs while standing (the Pandolf load term), and a kilogram on the feet counts as five on the back. Cold adds 15% of the resting rate when cold and 30% when very cold, and shivering 64 kcal an hour for each °C of core below 36.5, at most 4 × BMR more; digestion burns a tenth of what is absorbed; illness raises the resting rate (fever 10%, scurvy 20%). A day by the fire comes to about 2,300 kcal, a working day in camp about 3,300, a day hauling through snow 4,500, as soldiers measured in the Arctic (4,250 to 4,900 a day). The Messenger's Food page shows the day's burn by cause: Rest, Move, Load, Work, Cold, Digest.
- **Skills:** Fire-making (bow-drill strokes), Woodcutting (logs chopped and split), Butchering (dressing clicks and hides scraped) and Archery (arrows loosed at full draw) rise with use through five levels at 8, 32, 128 and 512 uses, each level cutting the time and kcal of its action by an eighth: an ember in fewer strokes, logs chopped and split faster, fewer dressing clicks and quicker scraping, a full draw sooner and more arrows recovered. A level rising says so on the action bar ("Woodcutting 3"); the Messenger's Body page shows each skill's level. Skills are the survivor's: a new survivor after extraction or death starts at level 1.
- **Weight follows the balance:** the day's surplus or deficit moves the fat reserve, 7,700 kcal to the kilogram. A survivor arrives at 77 kg and 1.80 m (BMI 23.8); at BMI 17 they are extracted (S10). Forge Labs went from 170 lb to 147.6 lb over his year.
- **Energy replaces hunger**: lightning bolts where the hunger bar was. Work drains it (32 kcal a kg of work above standing empties it), sleep restores it, food, cold and the pack's load change it. Low energy slows chopping, stops sprinting ("Too tired to sprint"), makes the survivor stumble and fall, black out, and drown when swimming in deep water.
- **Strength** follows body weight and energy: it decides whether a heavy bow draws fully.
- **Water:** about 3 L a day, drunk from the Water Bottle (1 L) and the Camp Pot (2 L), which both fill at lakes and rivers. The sea is salt: the water of oceans, beaches and stony shores fills nothing ("Sea water is salt: fill it at a lake or river"), and a vessel already holding water drinks its own when aimed at it. The Camp Pot set on a lit campfire boils its water in about 4 minutes, and the bottle fills from it; unboiled water gives a gut infection 40% of the time. Dehydration knocks the survivor out, a heart lost each time until they drink.
- **Health** comes back a heart every 2 game hours while energy and water are above a quarter and no infected wound or burn is open, twice as fast asleep.
- **Conditions,** durations in game days:

  | Condition | From | Does | Ends |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Infected wound | 15% of hits that hurt, as in Forge Labs' earlier mod | Fever: +10% kcal burn, no healing; after 2 days health drains | A bandage halts it and it heals 2 days later; the Med Kit at once |
  | Blisters | Walking with wet feet | No sprinting | 1 dry day, a bandage or the Med Kit |
  | Frostnip, frostbite, severe frostbite | Core below 35 °C, or a bare head at -15 °C and colder | Frostbite: slower, no sprinting; severe: health drains until death | Each stage falls back after 2 hours by a fire |
  | Burns | Fire, lava, a campfire | No healing | 2 days, halved by a bandage; the Med Kit at once |
  | Scurvy | 8 days without vitamin C | +20% kcal burn, wounds heal at half speed | Berries, spruce tea, liver or raw fish; cooking destroys vitamin C |
  | Food poisoning | Foods the data marks, spoiled food | Vomiting: the stomach's food and 1 L of water are lost | 1 day; the Med Kit at once |
  | Gut infection | 40% of unboiled drinks | Water loss doubled, half the kcal absorbed | 2 days; the Med Kit at once |
  | Parasites | 60% of raw meat eaten; fish frozen through is safe | A quarter fewer kcal absorbed | Only the Med Kit |
  | Exhausted | 2 days awake | Energy can't rise above half | Sleep |

  Too warm (core above 37.5 °C) is no condition: the survivor sweats, losing water and getting wet. A bandage is two plant fibre, which grass and ferns drop; the Med Kit holds 4 uses, each curing every condition it cures at once.
- **Inventory:** the hotbar and the rows a pack opens. Every survivor arrives wearing a Canvas Pack in the back slot beside the armour, which opens one row of 9 above the hotbar, and the Binoculars in the neck slot above it (S8); the Hide Pack, made from cured hides, opens two. Rows no pack opens stay dark ("Needs a pack", "Needs a hide pack"), and a pack's items stay in it when it comes off or is dropped.
- **Bulk:** how many of a thing fit in one slot follows its bulk, a slot being about a pocket's worth: logs 4, cobblestone and firewood 8, planks, rocks and cordage 16, sticks 32, plant fibre, hooks and pilot bread 64, raw meat and fish 4, tools and carcasses 1. Every item of every jar has its size (`balance.toml` `[bulk]`), so a pack of logs fills after a few slots while hooks hardly take room, and the weight counts on top. What is carried weighs; above 16 kg energy drains faster and the action bar shows "Pack too full". Mouse Tweaks drags and scrolls items between slots, and a middle click sorts the inventory under the cursor (Inventory Sorter): a chest, a cache or the Hide Pack's row.
- **Edge cases:**
  - A player offline, in creative or in spectator: their body stands still until they return to survival.
  - A night slept through: every skipped hour counts as sleep, burning and drinking at rest.
  - Two players eating from one cache: each eats their own ration from it.
  - `/time set`: the calendar follows Ecliptic Seasons' day count, never the clock, and the body lives only the ticks that pass.
  - A sort in the inventory: the rows a missing pack keeps dark stay empty.
  - A stack bigger than its item's bulk, from a world made before, stays as it lies; moving it splits it to the size.
  - A seated survivor: sneaking stands them up, a bear charge included; a broken seat stands them up; one who logs out seated joins standing; one seat to a block.

### S2 The year

- **48 days** of Ecliptic Seasons, 2 days to each of its 24 solar terms; a game day is 20 minutes, so a year is about 16 hours. Day 1 is 3 May, the last day of Grain Rain; one game day stands for about 7.6 calendar days, and dates are shown as dates (Day 9 · 2 Jul).
- Every duration (spoilage, seasoning, healing) counts game days.
- A date is 3 May plus (day - 1) × 365/48 days, rounded down: Day 9 · 2 Jul, Day 31 · 17 Dec. From Year 2 on, dates carry the year (Year 2 · Day 31 · 17 Dec).
- Daylight follows Anchorage, 61° N, through Ecliptic's day length per solar term: about 19.4 h of light at midsummer (16 minutes of a game day) and 5.5 h at midwinter (4.6 minutes).
- On 3 May the year's summary shows for the team, and play goes on into year 2; nothing ends with the year. It opens at each survivor's first quiet moment (on the ground, out of water, not hurt for 8 s, no screen open); someone offline sees it when they next join.

### S3 Weather and the forecast

- **The mod's own weather**, one sky for the whole world. Each game day is drawn from the world seed and the day: how far it drifts from S5's climate curve (by about 8 °C in winter and 2 °C in summer, half of it carried into the next day, so cold snaps bring lows to about -35 °C in the coldest places), its cloud, its wind and its direction, and at most one spell of rain or snow, 2 to 16 hours long, light, moderate or heavy. Wet days come as often as Anchorage's days with precipitation (NOAA 1991-2020: 5 in 30 in April, 15 in September). A thunderstorm is a rain day from June to August, one in four.
- **Rain or snow follows the air** where it falls: snow at +1 °C and colder, so it can rain at the beach while it snows in the grove above. Ecliptic's snow on the ground builds up while snow falls near sea level and melts on days above freezing.
- **A blizzard** is snow with wind of 56 km/h or more (the US weather service's threshold): outdoors the fog closes in to about 32 blocks and the light turns grey, and Distant Horizons' far view is fogged with it. The Complementary shader draws its own fog and ignores the game's, so under it a white-out of the blizzard's grey closes over the screen as the blizzard comes on instead.
- **Wind chills the body** in the open (the wind-chill formula of Environment Canada and the US weather service, above 4.8 km/h and at 10 °C and colder); a roof within 4 blocks, dense canopy included, takes the wind off (S5).
- **Lightning** flashes, thunders and hurts what it hits, but sets no fire and brings no skeleton riders.
- **The forecast** covers tomorrow, +2 and +3 days, issued each morning at 06:00 when the day turns: the real coming day with an error that grows each day further (2 °C more per day), a chance of rain or snow that drifts towards the season's usual chance, and the high and low where the survivor stands.
- **How it is held:** rain and thunder are vanilla's two world switches, which Ecliptic only reads; its own seasonal rain is off (`UseSolarWeather`), and the mod sets the switches each tick to its day. Ecliptic's rain-or-snow choice and its weather effects are answered by mixins from the mod's air.
- **Edge cases:** sleeping through a spell, `/time set` and `/backcountry day` give whatever that day and hour has; an empty server holds nothing; `doWeatherCycle` off leaves the mod's weather going and stops Ecliptic's snow on the ground changing; `/weather` is refused ("The weather follows the forecast") and a command block's the same; every survivor sees one sky, but the temperature, rain or snow and the alerts are each one's own spot.
- Animals react (fewer about in rain) with the spawn director in M6, and fishing changes with the weather in M7.

### S4 The Satellite Messenger

- A hand-held device that fills the screen (`interface.md` I2), its header the day, the date and the time of day, with five pages:
  - Tasks: the year's tasks in chapters, each with its steps (S11).
  - Weather: the air and how it feels in the wind, precipitation now or later today with its hours, wind and direction, cloud, today's high and low here; then tomorrow, +2 and +3 days, each with its chance of rain or snow, high, low and wind.
  - Warmth: core temperature, comfort and trend, and what sets them: fire, shelter (open, open in the wind, or roofed), clothing, wetness.
  - Food: kcal eaten and burned today with the burn by cause, protein, carbs and fat, fullness, the team's ration, the next meal, the team's food stores in days.
  - Body: weight and BMI, energy, water, the load carried, hours awake, conditions with their cure, skills.
- A value in warning says what helps in one line under it: a cold core, an open sky in the wind, wetness, food stores under 2 days, a BMI within 1 of extraction, low energy or water, a load over capacity.
- **Alerts**, each survivor's own: snow, a blizzard or a thunderstorm tomorrow (a chance of 50% or more here), frost tonight (tonight freezes here after a night above 0 °C), weight dropped (2 kg below where it last alerted; weight regained raises the mark), body wasting (BMI below 18.5, the WHO's underweight line). The weather alerts last until 06:00, weight dropped a day, body wasting until the BMI is back above it at a new day. Bears feeding heavily and moose rut begun come with M6's seasonal events.
- **A new alert** beeps once while the survivor carries a Messenger, and an amber light shows on its icon until it is opened.
- **Edge cases:** a messenger lost or broken comes again from the Supply Crate at the drop-off, which stands there from the world's first day and cannot be broken: a survivor carrying no messenger right-clicks it and takes a new one ("You already carry a Satellite Messenger" otherwise), and one wearing no pack takes a Canvas Pack the same way. Unread alerts wait for a messenger carried again; alerts missed while offline do not beep; an extracted or dead survivor's alerts go with them; a teammate's messenger shows its carrier's alerts; the survivor stands still while it is open.

### S5 Temperature and clothing

- Ambient temperature follows a climate curve, Anchorage's monthly normal highs and lows through the year and the day, shifted by the biome and by height; S3's weather drifts from it and cloud narrows the swing between day and night. Shelter (a roof within 4 blocks), wind in the open, wetness, fire heat and clothing insulation set the core temperature; rain and snow wet the survivor by their intensity.
- **Breath** shows as a puff of steam (Subtle Effects) where the air the survivor stands in is 7 °C or colder, the figure usually given for visible breath, and never in bright light from a fire or a lamp; a teammate's breath follows the air where they stand.
- The survivor arrives in clothes of their own; hide clothing adds insulation: the Hide Hat, the Hide Poncho (the lighter chest piece, worn instead of the coat), the Hide Coat and the Hide Pants.
- **Snowshoes** (rawhide strips and sticks) are worn on the feet for deep snow: snow 4 layers deep or more, or a snow block underfoot, halves a survivor's speed without them, and 256 blocks waded without them brings frostnip.
- **The Headlamp** arrives in the kit (S10) and is worn on the head in place of a hat, so in deep cold it is light or warmth. It is a crank lamp: holding use with it in hand winds it, a quarter hour of light for each second wound at 2 kcal, up to 8 hours. Worn, it lights the way through LambDynamicLights while its charge lasts, dimming from -20 °C to a quarter of its light at -40 °C; its tooltip tells the light left. Its charge runs only while worn by a survivor in the world: offline, in a chest or in hand it keeps; a dead or extracted survivor's lamp stays in their pack; teammates see its light.

### S6 Fire and wood

- **Felling:** bare hands get nothing from a log, as they get nothing from stone: a log takes an axe. The first axe is the stone axe: a Rock, a Stick and a Cordage, the knapped rock lashed to its handle, as are the stone pickaxe, shovel and hoe. Loose rocks lie on the ground, four of them making a cobblestone for building and the furnace, and each twig makes a stick; wooden tools are not made.
- **Planks are hewn:** a log stripped with the axe (a right-click) and the axe in the crafting grid make 4 planks, the axe losing 1 durability and the survivor the work of a chop; a log by hand makes none.
- **Materials:** stripping a birch log with an axe, standing or laid, peels off a Birch Bark; two Birch Bark and a plant fibre make a Tinder Bundle, four plant fibre make Cordage, and two sticks, a Cordage and a Softwood make the Bow Drill, good for 16 fires.
- **The Campfire** is built from three sticks over three logs and stands cold until it is lit. Vanilla's campfire, which burns forever, is never made.
- **Lighting:** right-click a cold campfire with the Bow Drill while carrying a Tinder Bundle and 2 sticks of kindling, and the bow-drill game opens. Saw with ◀ and ▶ (A and D) in turn until the friction makes an ember, then blow on it with Space to hold its glow in the band until the tinder flames: blowing too hard scatters the ember, too softly lets it die, and either way the sawing starts again. Rain on an open fire and a wet survivor cool the ember faster, and every stroke costs work. The tinder and the kindling are used only when the fire catches, and the kindling burns a quarter hour. Flint and steel, made later from flint and iron, lights it at once from the same tinder and kindling. Without them: "Needs a tinder bundle and 2 sticks".
- **Feeding:** a right-click with firewood puts a piece into the queue ("Fuel queue 3/4"), and green wood is refused ("Too green to catch").
- **The campfire screen** opens on a right-click with anything else that does not cook, or with firewood when the queue is full: a 4-slot fuel queue burning from the left, the heat on a bar, Low, Steady and Hot, burning and queued time, the coals and a checklist (`interface.md` I4). Low burns wood at half the rate, Hot at twice; the fire gives 8, 16 or 24 °C where it stands. Raw food still cooks on top of a lit fire, four pieces at a time; the furnace smelts iron, sand, clay and logs but cooks no food, so meat cooks over the fire, stews in the Cooking Pot, fish filleted on the fire or whole in the Smoker.
- **Coals:** each hour of burning banks an hour of coals, 8 at most. With an empty queue the fire settles to glowing coals that give 4 °C and burn down an hour an hour, and firewood queued while they last catches without the bow drill. "Coals banked" is ticked from 2 hours.
- **Packed too full:** a Hot fire with all four slots queued throws sparks: each hour, an even chance that a flammable block within 2 blocks catches, and vanilla's fire spreads from there; the screen warns "Packed too full: sparks may catch". A shovel puts a fire out, coals and all, and so does water.
- **Firewood** is Softwood (spruce) or Hardwood (birch and the other broadleaf trees): a log with its bark and an axe in the crafting grid split into 2 pieces, the axe losing 1 durability and the survivor the work of a chop. Each piece carries its seasoning, in its tooltip and as a bar on its slot: fresh Softwood is 50% seasoned and fresh Hardwood 25%, and below 50% wood is "Too green to catch" and the queue refuses it. A Softwood piece burns 1 hour at Steady and a Hardwood piece 2 hours with a quarter more heat, and a piece gives heat in proportion to its seasoning. **Dead spruces**, grey and bare poles 4 to 9 logs tall, stand scattered in the taiga, one in about every four chunks; a Dead Spruce Log splits into Softwood already seasoned through, so a first fire can burn hot from day 1 for whoever finds one.
- **Drying:** firewood laid on the ground (a right-click with it) stacks into a woodpile of up to 8 pieces that dries 12.5% a day, half that below freezing and not at all while rain or snow falls on it, so a roof keeps it drying. Carried or kept in a chest, wood keeps its seasoning. A right-click on a woodpile with an empty hand takes its top piece back, with the action bar telling the pile's pieces and seasoning.
- Trees fall whole (Panda's Falling Trees) and leave their logs to be split.
- **Torches and lanterns burn down.** A placed torch burns 36 game hours (30 real minutes) and a lantern 72 (60 real minutes), counted only while their land is loaded and for the hours slept; rain on an open torch burns it twice as fast, as on the campfire. A right-click with an empty hand tells what is left ("Torch: 20 h left"); a right-click with charcoal tops a torch up, with Rendered Fat a lantern. A torch that runs out burns away; a lantern hangs dark as an Empty Lantern until Rendered Fat lights it again, placed or in the crafting grid. A torch or lantern in hand burns too, a bar on its slot showing what is left, one of a stack at a time, so a night walk costs a torch, a lantern's fat or the Headlamp's winding; carried in the pack, they keep. A torch taken down keeps what it has left. Candles are not made (no honeybees, S12).
- **Edge cases:** a torch picked up keeps its hours and stacks only with torches holding as many; a survivor offline, in creative or dead burns nothing in hand; a torch placed from a half-burnt one carries its hours, and the next of the stack starts full; teammates refill each other's lights; rain on an open fire burns its wood and its coals twice as fast, and with an empty queue it settles to coals as anywhere; a fire or a woodpile in an unloaded chunk waits, while a night slept beside it burns and dries the hours slept; teammates at one campfire share one queue and one setting; a survivor who closes the bow-drill game, dies or is extracted in it keeps the tinder and has burned the work; a fire a teammate lit first keeps the tinder ("It is already burning"); a fire lit by a fire charge or a flaming arrow burns only what is queued.

### S7 Food, cooking and stores

- **Spoilage:** every food that spoils carries its age, which grows with the temperature where it lies: a day a day at 10 °C, twice as fast for every 10 °C warmer, half as fast for every 10 °C colder, and 32 times slower below freezing. Raw meat and fish keep 2.5 days at 10 °C (1 to 2 days in summer, 60 and more frozen), cooked meat 4, stews 2, bread 8, berries 4, roots and apples a month and more; pilot bread, pemmican and dried kelp never spoil. A food's tooltip reads "Spoils in 2.3 days here (9 °C)" by the air where the survivor stands, and once spoiled "Spoiled": eating it is food poisoning for certain. Food carried ages with its survivor; food in a chest, a barrel or a cache ages by its own spot, a chest's counted up whenever it is opened and a cache's every second while it is loaded.
- **Pots:** the Camp Pot hangs over the fire by its bail to boil water (S1). The Cooking Pot, five iron ingots on its own iron legs, stands on a campfire: water poured in from a bottle or the Camp Pot (2 L at most), foods added with a right-click (6 at most, an empty hand takes the last back), and after an hour over a lit fire the foods of a recipe become a stew, served into a bowl. Its stews are vanilla's, cooked here instead of in the crafting grid: mushroom stew (two brown mushrooms; a red one with a brown cooks a stew marked "Poisoned" that poisons whoever eats it), rabbit stew and beetroot soup, each serving using 0.25 L of the pot's water and giving it back to the survivor as drink. Rendering fat comes with M6's animal fat.
- **Mushrooms** are food: 12 kcal, the red one, a fly agaric, poisoning every second eater, cooked or not. Mushroom stew is 180 kcal and hydrates.
- **Spruce tea:** breaking spruce leaves with a knife drops 1 or 2 Spruce Needles. Needles put into a Camp Pot of plain water ("Needles in: it steeps as the pot boils") turn it into Spruce Tea when it boils, drunk like water from the pot or a bottle, with 128 mg of vitamin C a litre, the northern cure for scurvy when the berries are gone.
- **Birch sap:** a Birch Tap (a stick carved with the knife), set on the side of a birch log, gathers sap in April and May, a quarter litre at a time up to 2 L, the spout dripping; a right-click with the Water Bottle or the Camp Pot takes it ("No sap yet"; from June "The sap has stopped"). Birch sap drinks like water with 40 kcal a litre, and 2 L of it kept boiling in the Camp Pot for 960 s (about 19 game hours) boil down to a Birch Syrup of 80 kcal. A tap falls when its log goes.
- **Frozen fish:** fish lying at -16 °C or colder for a day, carried, cached or on the ground, is "Frozen through" and carries no parasites raw, keeping the vitamin C cooking would take; raw meat is no safer frozen.
- **Berries:** salmonberries ripen in June and July, the first fruit after arrival; blueberries in July and August; lingonberries (the vanilla berry bush, renamed) in August and September; highbush cranberries from September, keeping their berries on the bush into March. Baneberries, white with a black eye, ripen from July to September and poison whoever eats them (1 in 1), so a forager has to tell them apart. Salmonberry, highbush cranberry and baneberry bushes grow in the taiga and the forests, blueberries in the taiga. A bush grows only in its season and on days above freezing, regrows after picking within the season, drops what is left when its season ends, and scratches no one. Every edible one carries vitamin C against scurvy.
- **The Smoker**, built from 2 cordage, 3 logs, a campfire and 3 cobblestone: 4 hanging slots for meat and fish and 2 firewood slots for wood that catches. It smoulders a piece four times as long as a fire at Steady; its smoke is Cold, Smouldering for its first hour, then Rising, and after 12 hours of rising smoke the hung food comes out cooked and smoked, fresh, keeping 64 times as long as cooked: about 4 months at 20 °C and years frozen.
- **The Food Cache**, 7 planks and a cordage: 18 slots, and above them the usable food in kcal and the days it lasts at the team's ration, the ration itself (Lean, Normal, Feast), the survivor's fat reserve in kcal (what they can lose before extraction), how fast food spoils at its spot ("Spoils ×0.5 here (3 °C)"), and, holding 1,024 kcal of meat or fish or more, "Scent attracts scavengers" for M6's bears, wolves and wolverines. Usable food is food that is neither raw, spoiled, poisonous nor contaminated.
- **Rations:** at 06:00 and 18:00 every survivor in the world eats half the team's daily ration, Lean 1,280, Normal 1,600 or Feast 2,048 kcal a meal, from the team's caches within 64 blocks, soonest to spoil first, or from what they carry when no cache is in reach; the action bar tells it ("Dawn ration: 1,024 kcal", "Dusk ration: 600 of 1,024 kcal", "No food for the dawn ration"). Rations eat only usable food, and a full stomach stops a meal ("too full for more"). The Messenger's Food page shows fullness, the ration, the next meal and the team's food stores in days.
- **Mice:** at each dusk, every cache holding food has a 1 in 4 chance of mice: they eat 256 kcal of it, leave Mouse Droppings in a free slot and contaminate one stack ("Contaminated by mice"), which poisons whoever eats it, and every survivor's Messenger alerts "Mice in a cache" for a day. Deadfalls and mouse traps near a cache keep them off with M7.
- **Farming in real seasons:** crops grow only from May to August, in air of 8 °C or warmer, an eighth as fast as vanilla's, so wheat sown in May or June ripens in late August and the season leaves time for one planting a year. One seed in four does not germinate and is gone at its first growing chance; a night below freezing kills a crop that is not yet ripe. Bone meal grows nothing, crops, saplings and bushes alike; it still makes white dye.
- **Edge cases:** a cache in an unloaded chunk counts in the stores as it was left and catches up its spoiling when loaded again; offline, dead or extracted survivors eat no ration, and one offline at 06:00 does not eat it later; a night slept through dawn serves the dawn ration on waking; teammates at one cache share it, each eating their own ration; a teammate changing the ration changes it for the team; a ration with no cache in reach eats carried food; an emptied or broken cache leaves the stores; spoiled food in a cache still draws mice.

### S8 Hunting

- **The spawn director** spawns every animal. Every 30 seconds, for each survivor and each animal on its list (`data/backcountry/data_maps/entity_type/animal.json`), it rolls the animal's opportunities a day (a hare 4, a moose 0.5, a grizzly 0.125), halved while it rains or snows, doubled in the animal's season and, for scavengers (bears, wolves, wolverines), doubled while a cache within 64 blocks carries a scent. A roll that comes up places the animal's group 32 to 64 blocks away in its habitat (by water for the beaver, the loon and the waterfowl), out of the survivor's sight, while fewer than its cap are within 128 blocks. After a moose, a bear, a caribou or a beaver is taken the team gets no other of its kind for a while (a day for the big game, half a day for the rest). Every natural spawn is cancelled, monsters, patrols and wandering traders included, so a creature mod adds nothing until the list names its animals; traps draw on the same rolls with M7.
- **Seasonal events:** the moose rut in September doubles the moose and makes the Moose Call answer; bears feed heavily in August and September, doubling them. Each sends its alert on its first day ("Moose rut begun", "Bears feeding heavily"), lasting a day. Salmon running up waterfalls and spawned-out salmon unfit to eat come with River Fishing in M7.
- **Animals and where they live:** moose, grizzly (April to October), black bear (April to October), wolves in packs of 2 to 4, wolverine, beaver by water, caribou in herds on snowy ground, squirrels, snowshoe hares (vanilla rabbits, brown from May to October and white from November to April), ravens, chickadees, hawks, bald eagles, Canada geese, mallards and loons by water from May to September, gulls and seals on the beaches, orcas at sea, and the mod's own grouse (taiga) and ptarmigan (snowy ground, white in winter). The loon calls at dusk. How each acts, a wolf pack, a beaver's dam, a squirrel in a tree, is its mod's own.
- **Bears act their season:** a grizzly that sees a survivor within 16 blocks in May and June, after the den, or in August and September, feeding for winter, may come at them (one look in four, every second); in the other months it charges only a survivor who surprises it within 5 blocks. A bear at a carcass, a cache with a scent or its fishing stretch charges anyone within 8 blocks of it, whatever the month. One grizzly or black bear in four spawns as a sow with 1 or 2 cubs, who follow her: she charges anyone within 16 blocks of a cub, and a hurt cub calls her. A black bear is timid, fleeing from a survivor within 16 blocks, except a sow with cubs or a bear at food, which stands its ground and must be sprayed or driven off.
- **Bluff charges:** three charges in four are bluffs: the bear runs at the survivor, stops 2.5 blocks short with a roar and walks away, unless the survivor sprints away from it or hits it, which turns the charge into the attack. The Bear Spray ends any charge (it flees for 30 s).
- **The salmon run:** from July to September, bears spawn by rivers and lakeshores, fishing; every few game hours a fishing bear leaves a Half-eaten Salmon on the bank (1 kg of sockeye), which a survivor can take at their own risk, the bear defending its stretch as it would a carcass.
- **Mosquitoes:** from June to August, at night (20:00 to 05:00), with water within 8 blocks, no fire near, no rain and wind under 12 km/h, swarms bite: dark specks around the survivor, energy drains a little faster, and sleep among them rests half as well.
- **Binoculars** bring the land 8 times closer for spotting game. C looks through them while they are worn at the neck or lie anywhere in the hotbar, as holding use does with them in hand; the view is two circles without a crosshair, in first person even from over the shoulder, and the survivor walks slowly while looking. The island cannot make them: they come only in the kit (S10).
- **Weapons:** the Knife (flint, a stick and cordage; also the skinning knife), the axe, the bows and the Bear Spray; vanilla's swords, shields, bow and crossbow are not made (S12). Held right-click sprays the Bear Spray in a cone 4 blocks long, 8 bursts a can; a creature caught in it can't attack and flees for 30 s, and a survivor spraying point-blank is blinded for 4 s.
- **Bows:** a Bow Stave is cut from a hardwood log with the knife and seasoned on a lit campfire for 300 s; the Light Hunting Bow is a seasoned stave and a Bowstring (2 cordage) tillered with the knife, the Heavy Hunting Bow two seasoned staves, 2 rawhide strips and a bowstring. The heavy bow's arrows strike a quarter harder, but it draws only as far as the survivor's **strength** allows: strength is full at BMI 21 and half energy or more, and falls to nothing towards BMI 17 or an empty energy.
- **Arrows,** one per craft from a stick and a feather: blunt arrows for small game (no point to reach an organ), broadheads with a flint head (recovered 78% from blocks, 58% from animals) and bone arrows (70% and 48%). An arrow not recovered from a block stays stuck where it hit and can't be picked up; one recovered from an animal comes out when the carcass is dressed. Vanilla's arrow is not made.
- **A hit has to reach a major organ:** on an animal of 16 kg or more, an arrow that strikes its chest (40% to 80% of its height; the moose's 45% to 80%) kills it; any other hit by a survivor does half the damage and wounds it: "Wounded, it fled", it bleeds a trail of blood on the ground that fades in a day, and dies 2 hours later wherever it got to. Smaller animals fall to any good hit.
- **The Moose Call** (birch bark, rawhide strips, cordage and a stick): blown during the rut, a bull answers ("A bull answers") and comes from out of sight; out of the rut, "No answer". It can be blown once every 2 minutes.
- **Carcasses:** every animal on the list leaves a carcass where it dies. "Sneak + empty-hand click to carry. Set it down and use your knife to dress it." An animal of 64 kg or more is too heavy to carry ("Too heavy to carry: dress it here"), unless the survivor pulls a sled, which takes it whole ("Loaded onto the sled"). Dressing takes a click with the knife for every 8 kg of animal, 4 at least, each costing work and filling a bar on the action bar ("Dressing the Moose ▮▮▮▯▯▯▯▯▯▯"); without a knife: "Skinning knife required".
- **The Sled** (planks, rawhide strips and rope), set down like a boat and pulled on a lead (made from a Rope), carries 27 stacks or a whole carcass in its box and can't be ridden. It glides on snow and drags on bare ground, and what it carries counts in the survivor's burn while they walk: a quarter of its 16 kg and its load on snow, all of it on ground (S1). A survivor logging out or dying leaves the sled where it stands; a teammate can take the lead.
- **Cuts** per animal, each with its own kcal, fat and spoil time: meat (the mods' and vanilla's: moose ribs, raw game, raw venison, raw waterfowl, rabbit, grouse; bear meat is vanilla's beef renamed), heart, liver (with vitamin C), kidney, tongue, beaver tail, animal fat, game guts, pelts, bones, feathers and the moose's head. Raw moose meat: 1,600 kcal, 10 g fat, spoils in 2.3 days at 9 °C.
- **Hides:** a pelt or a rabbit hide scraped with the knife becomes a Scraped Hide; scraped hide and Rendered Fat (two animal fat in the Cooking Pot) make a Cured Hide; a scraped hide cut with the knife gives 4 Rawhide Strips. The Hide Hat, Poncho, Coat and Pants and the Hide Pack are made from cured hides.
- **Animals stay wild:** none breeds and none is tamed, whatever it is fed: a hare pen never grows, a grizzly thrown salmon stays a grizzly, a seal fed fish fetches nothing, and swimming beside orcas gives no Orca's Might.
- Every animal type, the mods' and vanilla's, is an entry in the animal data: habitat, months, group, opportunities a day, cap, weight, organ band, cuts, water, scavenging, season and rest.
- **Edge cases:** two survivors near each other each roll for their own surroundings, and the caps count every animal near; a wounded animal that leaves loaded chunks waits there and dies when loaded again, leaving its carcass at the trail's end; a carcass carried keeps its embedded arrows; an animal killed by another animal still leaves a carcass; a survivor offline, dead or extracted rolls nothing; a teammate dressing the same carcass adds to the same bar; C does nothing while the Binoculars lie only in the pack's rows; a screen opening stops the look; on the raft there is no slowdown; animals show only as far as the game sends them, so past that the far land shows empty; a teammate sees the arm raised when they are used in hand and nothing when looked through from the neck or the hotbar; the neck slot takes one pair and only Binoculars, and in creative it is hidden as the back slot is; a lost pair is gone, as the Headlamp is; a dead or extracted survivor's go into their pack, and a new survivor arrives wearing a pair.

### S9 Traps, fishing and water travel

- **Materials:** three cordage make Rope; an iron ingot draws into 4 Snare Wire ("bends into snares and hooks": a snare wire also bends into 2 of River Fishing's hooks); 4 snare wire make Wire Mesh; two sticks cut with the knife make 2 Trap Stakes; three sticks and a cordage make a Crossbeam.
- **Traps** are set on the ground and watched by the hour, drawing on the spawn director's rolls for their animals (their opportunities a day, a half share, doubled when baited, nothing out of season, outside the animal's habitat or while the team rests from its kind), so a caught hare counts like a hunted one:
  - the Small Snare (snare wire and stakes) takes hares, squirrels, grouse and ptarmigan;
  - the Large Snare (rope, 2 snare wire and stakes), set within 2 blocks of water, takes beavers;
  - the Deadfall (a crossbeam, stakes and a cobblestone) takes hares and squirrels, and keeps mice off;
  - the Mouse Trap (snare wire and a plank) keeps mice off: no cache within 8 blocks of a deadfall or a mouse trap is raided;
  - the Gill Net (rope and 4 cordage), set in still water, and the Tip-Up (2 sticks, a cordage and a snare wire), set over open water in the ice, take fish, two a day on average: grayling, northern pike, lake trout, Arctic char, burbot, whitefish, sockeye, coho and pink salmon, each of its own weight.
  A trap that holds something shows it sprung; a right-click takes the catch ("You take the Snowshoe Hare Carcass") or tells it is set ("Set, nothing yet", "Set and baited, nothing yet"). Snares and deadfalls take a Cricket as bait. The minnow trap is River Fishing's Bait Trap.
- **Bait:** worms turn up when grass or dirt is dug with a shovel within 12 hours after rain (River Fishing's worms, one dig in two); crickets jump from grass, ferns and tall grass cut in June to August (one in eight); live minnows and bait from fish and a hook are River Fishing's own.
- **Fishing** is River Fishing's, with Alaska's own fish only: Arctic grayling, northern pike, lake trout, Arctic char, Dolly Varden, rainbow and cutthroat trout, burbot, whitefish, sheefish, sculpin, smelt and the five salmon, and at sea Pacific cod, halibut, pollock, herring and flounder (`cuts.toml` `fish`; no other species finds water to live in, by rod, net or bait trap). Its tackle is what the island makes and a rod takes: the stick rod in open water and the winter rod through the ice, both without a reel, with their hooks, lines, primitive and winter rigs, lures and bait, the ice auger, the bait trap, the nets and keepnets. The kit's Mono Line and 4 hooks rig a Stick Rod (3 sticks) from the first day; string from the forest's cobwebs makes more line and snare wire more hooks. Every other rod needs bamboo, copper, diamond or a reel the island cannot make, and its electronics, aquariums and ponds stocked with roe and fry, fishing stall and contracts, and worm and maggot farms are cut (S12). The pack gives each catch its kcal by weight giving each catch its kcal by weight: 482 kcal, 50 g fat and 200 g protein a kilogram (a large raw pike: 3.9 kg, 1,879 kcal), raw fish carrying vitamin C against scurvy and spoiling in 2.5 days at 10 °C, cooked fish in 4. Its seasons follow the island's year (spring from March), and its barometer follows the island's sky: falling before rain, low under cloud and wind. A salmon landed in September or October is spawned out ("Spawned out, unfit to eat") and poisons whoever eats it. Every fish landed counts in the run's and the year's summary ("22 fish").
- **The Raft**, 5 logs and a rope (vanilla's bamboo raft, built of logs here), carries a survivor and a passenger; in wind above 24 km/h a moving raft can pitch its rider off ("The raft pitched and you fell off"), more often the stronger it blows.
- **Lake ice:** still water freezes where the air is -4 °C or colder and the ice thaws where it is above 1 °C, so the taiga's lakes freeze over in a cold spell and open again in spring; River Fishing's ice auger cuts a hole for the tip-up and the rod.
- **Edge cases:** a trap in an unloaded chunk waits, and one emptied in the meantime starts watching again; a trap holds one catch, so a full trap catches nothing more; a sprung snare keeps its carcass until it is taken, and a broken trap drops its catch and bait; teammates share every trap; a raft rider who falls in gets wet; ice formed under a survivor holds them.

### S10 The team, arrival and the end of a survivor

- Everyone in a world is one team; in singleplayer it is a team of one. Caches, rations, food stores in days and the spawn director's rations are the team's; the body, the messenger and the kit are each survivor's.
- **Voice:** teammates hear each other within 48 blocks, muffled through walls (Simple Voice Chat, Sound Physics Remastered); the microphone is open from the first join and V opens the voice menu, where push-to-talk can be chosen. A player without a microphone hears the others; a server whose voice port is closed leaves voice silent and the game plays on. Over a teammate's head shows when they are typing, have a screen open or are idle (What Are They Up To).
- Every survivor arrives at the drop-off on the main island's coast, facing the sea (`world.md` W4), with the starting kit, as a survivalist on the show Alone would pack it: the Canvas Pack and the Binoculars worn; in the hotbar the Satellite Messenger, Bear Spray, Water Bottle, Pilot Bread ×38 (100 kcal, 2.5 g fat each) and Camp Pot; in the pack the Sleeping Bag (used in hand on the ground, the survivor sleeps where they stand), a Mono Line, 4 hooks, the Med Kit and the Headlamp.
- At BMI 17 the survivor is extracted: their screen fades, they leave the world, and everyone reads "Roman was extracted at BMI 17.0". A survivor may also tap out whenever they choose, as on *Alone*, with SOS on the Messenger (`interface.md` I2): they leave the world as at BMI 17, and everyone reads "Roman tapped out on Day 31". At death they die. Whichever way, the player sees the run's summary (days, weight curve, kcal eaten, animals taken) and arrives again at the drop-off as a new survivor, in the same world with its seasons and camps. Beds and sleeping bags never move where a survivor arrives.
- **Edge cases:** an extracted, tapped-out or dead survivor's pack stays where they were, a container the team can open ("Roman's pack"), on the nearest ground within 8 blocks or the sea floor beneath; a survivor ending with nothing carried leaves none; leaving the game on the summary brings it back on the next join. SOS works only for a living survivor in survival, and only the server ends the run, once: a second press, a death or BMI 17 arriving while the question stands end it no more than once, whichever comes first; logging out with the question open ends nothing; teammates play on, the caches and rations staying the team's.
